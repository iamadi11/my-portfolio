Save File: the 3D build

The greybox draws the town flat. The real build is "HD-2D": a 3D diorama with pixel sprites, seen through the handheld's screen.

Look

  • The town is a low-poly 3D diorama (KayKit City Builder Bits or Kenney Suburban, CC0) rendered at 160×144 with a 4-colour palette shader, so 3D reads as pixel art.
  • Characters are pixel sprites billboarded into the 3D scene; tilt-shift depth of field like a model village.
  • The handheld itself is a 3D object in a soft studio; on desktop you can rotate it, the cartridge pops out when you save.
  • LCD shader: pixel grid, ghosting, a little backlight bleed.

Play

  • Doors are case studies; NPCs give short lines; the gym battle is a turn-based system-design fight (pick the right fix for the fault). Winning earns a skill badge.
  • Real save: position and badges persist; "Continue" on return.
  • Chiptune sound from CC0 packs; off until START.

Why phones win

Portrait is the native shape of a handheld. Touch buttons are big, nothing needs a keyboard, and it runs at 160×144: tiny GPU cost.

Recruiter path

"Read as a page" opens the plain résumé before anything loads; START → RÉSUMÉ inside the game does the same.
